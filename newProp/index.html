What "entertaining" means this week

Today's story, on the homepage

The content engine already writes something worth reading daily — give it a real spot on the site instead of only social media.

Visible interests

Reviving the interest tags makes a profile feel like a personality, not a form — and it's the same data Leo needs.

Favoriting that feels good

A small confirming animation on favoriting a shop costs an afternoon and makes the action feel worth repeating.

Empty states with a voice

"No events yet" is a dead end. "Nothing here yet — be the first to submit one" is an invitation.

Making it easy to love

Six principles for a collector who's often standing in a crowded show with one hand free.

Fast images, honestly

13MB of uncompressed PNGs (flagged in the original audit) means a slow load on show-floor wifi. WebP + lazy-loading isn't polish, it's the difference between someone waiting and someone leaving.

One tap, no forms

Favoriting and saving already work this way — protect that. Every new feature should ask "can this be one tap?" before it becomes a form.

Near me, by default

Lead with location-sorted results, not a directory someone has to filter down. The most relevant thing should be the first thing.

Never a dead end

Every empty screen is an invitation to act, not a wall. Same principle as the empty-states item above, applied everywhere — search results, no-favorites-yet, first login.

Built for the show floor

Assume one thumb, bad lighting, and patchy signal. Optimistic UI (show the favorite as saved instantly, sync after) matters more here than almost anywhere else.

Talk like a collector

"Profile 80% complete" is database language. "Add your first favorite shop" is collector language. Small wording choices are part of the product, not an afterthought.

Next — driving real interaction

After this week: five moves to earn repeat visits, in order.

Every one of these does double duty — it's a reason to come back, and it's a data point Leo eventually sells. Sequenced by build cost against payoff.

1

"Near you" feed

Events and shops ranked by distance + interest tags on the homepage, instead of a generic directory. Turns a browse into a reason to check back weekly.

2

Verified event check-in

Upgrade the existing "save an event" signal into a confirmed-you're-here action at the actual show. The single most valuable, hardest-to-fake number in any sponsorship report — and a nice "I was there" moment for the collector.

3

Weekly digest email

Curated from the content engine's daily picks. Brings back collectors who haven't opened the site in a while, and opens/clicks are their own engagement signal.

4

Shop & event reactions

Lightweight — a save or a quick reaction, not a full review system. Cheap to build, and surfaces which shops are actually well-regarded, not just listed.

5

Invite a collectorGROWTH

A simple share/referral mechanic. More users in a region is what makes the eventual report credible in the first place — this is the one Leo should push once the product side is ready.

How Leo brings in users

He doesn't need broad marketing — he needs to point people already near the hobby at the site.

Sequenced by how much it costs to try, not by size of eventual impact — start with what's free.

1

Ask existing consulting clients

The companies Leo already advises can point their own audience at KLLCTRS — a QR code at a sponsored booth, a link in a newsletter. Costs Leo one conversation, uses trust that already exists.

2

Work the shows he's already at

A physical sign-up moment (QR code, flyer, a person at a table) at shows like Dallas, where collector intent is at its highest point of the year.

3

Recruit shops as promoters

A shop listed on KLLCTRS benefits from exposure — ask them to display a sign-up code in-store. Shops have their own incentive to say yes, no cold pitch needed.

4

Trade visibility with sponsors

Companies being pitched the data product can also promote sign-up to their own customer base in exchange for being an early, favored voice in the reports — aligns their interest with growing the platform.

5

Let the content engine compoundONGOING
